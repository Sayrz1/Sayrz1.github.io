Salt & Gull

Fish & chips, Yorkshire coast

Proper chips. No nonsense.

The problem. Every Yorkshire chippy looks the same: red, white and stuck in 1985. Salt & Gull needed an identity that felt like the seaside today. Salty, funny, and impossible to miss on a phone.

The idea. One cheeky gull, one navy-and-yellow system, and headlines that sound like something a local would actually say. Sid the gull steals the show in every post, so you know the brand with the logo covered.

Drizzle

Sparkling soda, London

Properly fizzy.

The problem. Every British soda brand runs from the rain: grey skies, grey moods, grey cans. It rains in London most weeks, so pretending it doesn’t is the least honest thing a drink can do.

The idea. Drizzle owns the weather instead. A can that rains pink, a little cloud that smiles through every shower, and headlines a Londoner would actually say. The forecast becomes the flavour.

Naseeg

نسيج

Arabic coffee & dates, Riyadh

حيّاك في مجلسنا Welcome to our majlis.

The problem. Riyadh has a speciality café on every corner, but none of them feels like a majlis. Arabic coffee deserves a brand with the warmth of the gathering itself, not another minimal beige logo.

The idea. The Sadu weave is the identity. The coffee bag opens into a majlis tent, and every post is a seat at the gathering: red, saffron and black, with the dallah always in the frame.

Ghasaq

غسق

Oud perfume house, Dubai

Your presence lingers.

The problem. Every oud house in the Gulf sells the same thing: dark bottles, gold caps, heritage clichés. Ghasaq needed to feel like the night itself. Quiet, expensive, impossible to ignore once you’ve smelled it.

The idea. Scent as smoke. One gold smoke trail curls through every image, off the dune, around the bottle, behind her at the wedding, so you know the brand with the logo covered and the night does the selling.

Wathba

وثبة

Modest activewear, Riyadh & Dubai

Nothing stops you. Not even the heat.

The problem. Modest activewear in the Gulf either looks like gym kit with extra fabric, or fashion that can’t survive a real workout. Active women wanted kit that performs in 45° heat and looks like it means it.

The idea. Wathba is built for speed. One off-black kit with a lime pulse, slanted type that leans into the run, and motion streaks in every frame. Nothing stops you, not even the heat.

Rand

رند

Luxury villa communities, Riyadh

بيتك.. يبدأ من هنا Home starts here.

The problem. Every luxury villa developer in Riyadh sells the same things: square metres, marble finishes and a render of an empty house. Rand needed to sell the life that happens inside it.

The idea. The mashrabiya is the identity, the carved screen that gives a Saudi home its light, shade and privacy. It frames every post in golden-hour light, with one line underneath: home starts here.

Plug & Brew

EV charging hubs with a café, UK

Plug in. Put the kettle on.

The problem. Charging an EV on the motorway is a dead wait. Drivers stand in the rain by a charger for half an hour with nothing to do and nowhere warm to go.

The idea. Plug & Brew turns the wait into a proper British break, with a warm café lounge at every hub. The teal charging ring is the logo, and the whole stop fits in one line: plug in, put the kettle on.

Greggs

Unofficial social campaign, UK

Ordinary, done brilliantly.

The problem. Greggs is the nation’s favourite bakery, but it’s so everyday that people walk past it without a second thought. The job is to make the ordinary feel worth noticing.

The idea. “Ordinary, done brilliantly” treats the commute, the tea break and the cold bus stop like they matter, with the sausage roll as the hero of every small moment.

Monzo

Unofficial social campaign, UK

Money that keeps up with real life.

The problem. Banks all sound the same: formal, distant, slightly scary, so people switch off before they’ve even started. The job is to make money feel like something you can talk about, not something to dread.

The idea. “Money that keeps up with real life” puts Monzo quietly in the background of the moments that matter, like the bill split, the festival bar and the late-night shop, with the hot coral card as the one thing you always notice.

Unofficial concept work. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Monzo.
